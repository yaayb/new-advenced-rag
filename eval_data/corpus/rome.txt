The Roman Aqueducts
Roman engineers built aqueducts to carry fresh water into cities using gravity alone. Channels sloped gently, often by only a fraction of a percent, over distances of many kilometres. Arched bridges carried the water over valleys, as seen in the Pont du Gard in southern France. Settling tanks removed sediment before water reached public fountains and baths. Rome itself was served by eleven aqueducts at its peak, supplying an estimated one million inhabitants.
